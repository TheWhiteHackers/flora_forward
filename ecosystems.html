Louisana's

Ecosystems

Louisiana is celebrated for its incredibly diverse wildlife and natural environments. Stretching from expansive marshlands to its numerous lakes and rivers, the state provides a thriving, unique ecosystem capable of supporting a vast array of species.

Marshes

Covering roughly 10,700 square miles, Louisiana's coastal marshes account for about 12% of all coastal wetlands in the United States. These environments are built upon sediments deposited by the Mississippi River and serve as vital nursery grounds for many species of fish and shellfish.

Diamond-Backed Watersnake

Diamond-backed watersnakes are typically found occupying shallow, slow-moving bodies of water like swamps, rivers, and estuaries. Their diet relies heavily on amphibians like frogs, as well as small to medium-sized fish.

Catfish

Native to Louisiana, channel catfish can be found in almost every type of freshwater system across the state, including swamps, natural lakes, and rivers. They are opportunistic omnivores that feed on a varied diet of frogs, crustaceans, insects, and small fish.

Prairie

The Cajun Prairie is a unique grassland ecosystem dominated by native grasses and a wide variety of broad-leaved wildflowers. In a healthy prairie, tree growth is naturally restricted by factors like seasonal droughts, natural fires, and grazing animals.

Prairie Kingsnake

The prairie kingsnake makes its home in the mixed pine woods and upland flatwoods of central, northern, and southwestern Louisiana. They maintain a stable population within the state by hunting small rodents, shrews, lizards, and other small snakes.

Elliot's Short-Tailed shrew

Elliot's short-tailed shrew, scientifically known as Blarina hylophaga, is a small mammal characterized by its slate grey fur and short tail. It is highly adaptable and can thrive in various habitats, including woodlands, agricultural fields, and grasslands.

Woodland

Louisiana's swamp and bottomland hardwood ecosystems are wetland areas characterized by regular flooding and dense woody vegetation. In these southern habitats, the most prominent native tree species include the Tupelo gum and the Bald Cypress.

Western Ribbonsnake

The western ribbonsnake is a specific species of garter snake belonging to the Colubridae family. It is widely distributed across the central and southern portions of the United States, extending down through Mexico and into Central America.

Leopard Frog

Southern leopard frogs are highly adaptable amphibians with a massive geographic range that spans from central Texas to New York. They prefer to live near shallow freshwater habitats and feed on a wide variety of both terrestrial and aquatic insects.
